# Music by 0001

A small open-source arrangement player prototype inspired by Ezo and Live. Six tracks run horizontally in 20px-high rows. Pulse and Piano also start horizontally. Every track starts with a steady four-bar loop, 640px wide at the default scale. Piano is taller to show its two voices in notation view. Each has an 80×20px name chip overlaid on the first four grid squares of its waveform lane, revealed on hover or keyboard focus. There is no separate name column or extra name row. The waveforms are measured from real audio.

[Play Music](https://music.0001.dev) · [Source code](https://github.com/0001-labs/music)

Open the published site, or double-click `serve.command` to run it on this Mac: it serves the app and opens it in Safari (close its window to stop). Safari, like every browser, will not load the audio when `dist/index.html` is opened straight from the disk, so the page says so instead of waiting; serve it with the launcher or run `python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory dist` from this folder. A notation editor is at `/notation`.

Play follows each track from left to right or top to bottom, then loops at its visible end. The default display scale is 40px per beat: half a bar occupies four 20px grid squares, and a full bar occupies eight. The default 640px lanes contain four bars. Each repeats its first two-bar groove twice, without moving through contrasting clip variants. This test arrangement uses a fresh saved-layout key so previous stretched/vertical arrangements do not replace these defaults; subsequent edits to this layout are saved normally. Each track has an independent playback position and audio source. The play/stop button before its name controls only that track; Stop resets its position so the next play starts from the beginning, and clicking its waveform seeks only that track. The whole looping track lights up when enabled, including while waiting to join playback. New starts, resumes, and running seeks join on the next shared bar boundary, and playback positions snap to the nearest bar. The global Play/Pause controls all tracks; Stop resets all positions. Tracks share the tempo, while their positions can differ. Each playing, audible track has its own one-pixel playhead inside its waveform lane, with no square marker. Muted and excluded tracks show no playhead. Click a waveform to seek. There is no numbered ruler. Playback and tempo controls sit together without gaps in the upper-right corner. Hover a track to reveal its 16×16px play/stop button and name at the start, with the M/S split control at the right end of a horizontal track or the bottom of a vertical track. M mutes a track; S solos it. Active play/stop and M/S buttons remain visible after the pointer leaves. Keyboard focus also reveals it, and touch devices keep it visible. Tempo runs from 70–160 BPM. Space plays and pauses; Stop resets the position. Drag anywhere on a waveform or its name to move the entire track smoothly. Nearby tracks slide aside to preview a free space; dropping snaps placement to four 20px grid squares (80px) on both axes. A click still seeks; Play/Stop and M/S stay clickable. Horizontal and vertical tracks can be placed in free grid space beside one another. Tracks stay separate after dropping: surrounding tracks move into nearby free positions, and existing saved overlaps are moved apart. Dragging near the viewport edge scrolls the canvas. Escape or a canceled drag restores the whole previous layout; Drag on empty grid space to marquee-select tracks. Shift-drag adds to the selection; Shift-click toggles a track. Arrow keys move selected tracks together by four grid squares, and dragging a selected track moves the group with surrounding tracks flowing aside. A plain track click selects it while still seeking its waveform. Click empty space or press Escape to clear selection. Placed clips select the same way: click one, Shift-click to add or remove others. From the keyboard, Shift+Arrow extends the selection to the nearest track or clip in that direction and the opposite arrow takes the last one back; an arrow with nothing selected picks a clip (the focused one, or the first or last on the board); Command/Ctrl+A selects every track and clip. Arrows then move the whole selection and Delete removes it, tracks and clips together as one Undo step. Drag the small corner handle to scale a track taller or wider in grid increments. This preserves its audio duration and loop; halving its displayed length halves the local playhead’s visual speed. The waveform retains one-pixel strokes as it scales. Drag the separate handle centered on the ending edge to trim or extend the loop in half-bar steps, at the current display scale. It sits at the right center of horizontal tracks and the bottom center of vertical tracks. Arrow keys on either handle perform its corresponding operation; Escape cancels. Beyond eight bars, the original arrangement repeats. Thin dashed lines across the waveform mark each repeat of the original eight-bar pattern and the final track loop end; they follow both display scaling and trimming in either orientation. Scaling and trimming cannot overlap other tracks. Focus the handle and use arrow keys to resize; Escape cancels a resize. Select tracks and press Delete or Backspace to delete them, or use Delete in the track context menu. Deleted tracks stop playing, leave global playback, and remain deleted after refresh. Undo restores their saved geometry, color, mute, and solo settings. Undo/redo covers whole drag gestures (including displaced neighbors), arrow moves, scaling, trimming, orientation, color, mute, solo, and deletion; canceling a gesture adds no history entry. Use the transport Undo/Redo buttons, Cmd/Ctrl+Z, Shift+Cmd/Ctrl+Z, or Ctrl+Y. New edits clear redo; history is kept for the current session. Right-click a track (or press Shift+F10 while focused) for a compact context menu: Make vertical / Make horizontal. Turning a track rotates its waveform and playhead while its audio continues. If the rotated shape is occupied, the track moves to the nearest free grid position. The same menu includes Ezo's nine note colors: white, red, orange, yellow, apple green, every green, zenith blue, sharp blue, and pink. A chosen color lightly tints the idle track and fills its whole active loop; waveform and playhead ink keep contrast. Existing saved placements are snapped to the four-square grid and moved apart if necessary. Positions, sizes, orientations, and chosen colors are saved locally in this browser; playback state stays in memory. The working surface contains only the transport and arrangement, with no footer. The Notation / Waveform button beside playback switches Kick, Snare, Hats, Bass, Keys, Pulse, and Piano between musical notation and measured waveforms. Drum symbols follow the actual hit patterns in each clip: oval heads for Kick and Snare, crossed heads for Hats, and rhythm stems/flags on expanded lanes. Air retains its waveform. The original Bach-inspired piano invention uses a treble melody and bass counterpoint; its notation follows the same note events used to synthesize its audio, with two staves in a tall lane. This view choice is saved locally and does not change playback or track placement.

Double-click empty grid space to start a white global playback strip moving right, behind the audio tracks. Shift-double-click starts a downward player. The starting position and playback strip placement snap to the same four-square (80px) grid as tracks. Each loops from that snapped position to the furthest track edge and has its own stop square. Both directions play horizontal and vertical tracks while respecting mute/solo. Tracks in the same orientation enter at their canvas positions; tracks in the other orientation start with the loop. Thin blue markers inside audible tracks follow their playback, including down vertical waveforms. Native audio loops continuously, and display scaling preserves audio duration. Multiple global players and independent track loops can run together. Global Pause freezes all players; Play resumes them, and global Stop removes them. Dragging empty space still selects tracks. Drag along the row above the board to draw a loop region: it starts on the square where the drag begins and runs to the square where it ends, in 80px steps, and the drag shows its length in bars. A plain click there draws nothing, and Escape cancels a drag.

Includes 32 original synthesized WAV loops. The eight tracks are Kick, Snare, Hats, Bass, Keys, Air, Pulse, and Piano. The original arrangement contains eight bars per track. Source tempo is 112 BPM, in A minor. Waveforms show measured amplitude peaks, resampled for the display size with consistent one-pixel strokes at two-pixel intervals in either orientation. The trim handle crops the waveform; the corner handle resamples the complete loop into its new visual length. Tempo changes alter pitch along with playback speed in this first prototype.

When the window has room beside the board, the whole menu (transport, sample explorer and effect map) stays open there, clear of the squares; in a narrower window it opens over the board from the Music chip and closes on a click elsewhere. Open Settings and the sample explorer lists 61 original loops: drums and percussion, bass, keys and pads, melody, voices, and textures and effects. Click one to hear it; drag it onto the grid to place it as a loop. Every sample is four bars at 112 BPM in A minor over Am, F, C and G, so any two fit together, and each loops without a seam. The samples are about 44 MB in all. Regenerate them, and the list that shows them, with `python3 scripts/generate_samples.py` (name samples to rebuild just those). Add a sample by writing a builder in `scripts/sample_drums.py`, `sample_tones.py` or `sample_textures.py` and giving it a group in `generate_samples.py`.

Regenerate the audio with `python3 scripts/generate_audio.py`. The generator uses only Python's standard library. The app runs directly as static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with no build step or external runtime dependencies.
